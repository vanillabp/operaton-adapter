### A new Operaton minor merges itself once the build is green

Proposed by story 2099. It replaces decision 4 in the part about a new minor. Decision 4 stays,
marked as superseded by this one.

**Why.** Decision 4 let Renovate raise a patch on its own and gave a new minor to a person. Three
things needed that person:

- The adapter uses engine classes under `.impl.`, which Operaton may change in any minor. The build
  compiles and tests the adapter against the new engine, so a change there makes the pull request
  red. A person adds nothing to that.
- The README named the version the adapter was tested with. It now points at the property
  `operaton.version` of the root POM and names no number.
- The recipe `rewrite/vanillabp-operaton.yml` wrote the minor into the root POM of `generated`.
  It no longer does: `bin/sync-from-camunda7.sh` takes the version from the root POM of `main`.

What nobody noticed with a person either: a new minor can bring an engine setting the adapter does
not pass on. Operaton 2.2 brought `jobExecutorAcquireWithSkipLocked`, and the adapter was built on
2.2 from its first commit without anybody seeing it. The job `client-configuration` of
`checks.yaml` now compares the settings of the two versions and opens an issue for each new one.

**What holds.**

- Renovate merges a patch and a minor of Operaton once the build is green. A major still needs a
  person, because it may change the database and the API.
- Nothing but the root POM names the Operaton version. Whoever adds a second place has to make it
  read the POM, or a check has to fail when the two differ.
- The rest of decision 4 holds: one line, the newest minor, no other minor in an application, and
  the nightly build against the next Operaton.

- `renovate.json`, the rule for a patch and a minor (no citation yet, add one).
- `README.md`, section "Supported Operaton version", which cites decision 4 today.
